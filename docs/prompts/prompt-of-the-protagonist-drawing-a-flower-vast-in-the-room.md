Question: Give me a list of camera angles and perspectives that can be used to capture the scene of the protagonist drawing a flower in the room, along with a brief description of each angle:

1. **Overhead Perspective:** Captures the scene from above, showing the protagonist's head and shoulders as she leans over her drawing, with a clear view of the flower and art supplies on the table.
2. **Low Angle View:** Captures the scene from a low angle, looking up at the protagonist, with a portion of the jar of water in the foreground and the room in the background, emphasizing the protagonist's focus and the cozy atmosphere of the room.
3. **Eye-Level View:** Captures the scene from the protagonist's eye level, showing her face and the flower she is drawing, with the room's details in the background, creating an intimate and relatable perspective.
4. **Close-Up Shot:** Focuses closely on the protagonist's hands as she draws the flower, highlighting the details of her drawing and the art supplies, while the background is softly blurred to emphasize her creative process.
5. **Wide-Angle Shot:** Captures the entire room, including the protagonist, the drawing, and all the cozy details, providing a comprehensive view of the environment and the protagonist's interaction with it.
6. **Bird's Eye View:** Similar to the overhead perspective but taken from a higher angle, giving a more expansive view of the room and the protagonist's position within it, emphasizing the spatial composition and the relationship between the protagonist and her surroundings.
7. **Dutch Angle (Tilted Shot):** Captures the scene with a tilted camera angle, creating a sense of whimsy and playfulness that complements the creative activity of drawing, while still showing the protagonist and the room's details in an engaging way.
8. **Point of View (POV) Shot:** Captures the scene from the protagonist's perspective, showing what she sees as she looks down at her drawing, with the flower in clear view and the art supplies around her, creating an immersive experience for the viewer.
9. **Medium Shot:** Captures the protagonist from the waist up, showing her focused expression and the flower she is drawing, while also including some of the room's details in the background to provide context.
10. **Over-the-Shoulder Shot:** Captures the scene from behind the protagonist, showing her shoulder and the flower she is drawing in the foreground, with the room's details in the background, creating a sense of intimacy and connection between the viewer and the protagonist's creative process.

## Prompt Rules:
1. **Core Subject & Medium First:** Start the prompt by immediately defining the artistic style/medium and the main subject or action.
2. **Natural Language Integration:** Do not use rigid camera tags (like "ISO 400", "f/1.4", "shot on 50mm lens"). Instead, translate technical camera angles, framing, and details into descriptive English phrases (e.g., "A wide-angle landscape shot," "Focusing sharply on the subject's face," "Seen from a low perspective looking up").
3. **Spatial Composition:** Clearly weave the layout into the narrative using positioning words like "In the foreground," "In the midground," "Against a background of."
4. **Atmosphere & Lighting:** Describe lighting, textures, colors, and mood naturally as adjectives modifying the environment rather than separate bullet points.
